<p>The first time I made a Cafe Cubano at home, the sugar sank straight to the bottom. No foam. No sweetness. Just a gritty mess. That’s when I realized: this drink isn’t about dumping sugar into espresso. It’s about building a structure — a sugar foam that floats on top and dissolves slowly as you sip. The technique is everything. Get it right, and you have a cup that’s bold, sweet, and balanced. Get it wrong, and you’re left with a sad, sugary puddle.</p>
<figure><img src="https://cdn.midjourney.com/c62d8dd3-baef-46c8-914b-4a3effbeb809/0_3.png" alt="A person stirring sugar and coffee in a small bowl to create espuma" /></figure>
<h2>What Makes a Cafe Cubano Different</h2><p>A Cafe Cubano starts with a concentrated coffee — typically espresso, but a strong moka pot brew works too. The magic is in the <em>espuma</em>: a whipped mixture of sugar and a tiny amount of coffee. The ratio matters. I keep my sugar at about 20 grams (around 1.5 tablespoons) for a double shot of espresso. That may seem like a lot, but it’s not cloying — the bitterness of the coffee cuts through. The sugar-to-coffee ratio is roughly 1:1.5 by weight. Too little sugar, and the foam won’t develop. Too much, and the drink becomes syrupy.</p><h2>The Espuma: How to Get It Right</h2><p>The espuma is the heart of this drink. Here’s the process I follow:</p><h3>1. Start with the Right Sugar</h3><p>Demerara sugar is ideal. Its coarse crystals have more surface area, which helps trap air during stirring. White granulated sugar works, but the foam will be thinner. Avoid powdered sugar — it dissolves too quickly and won’t whip.</p><h3>2. Add Just Enough Liquid</h3><p>Place the sugar in a small bowl. Add exactly one teaspoon of hot coffee — not water, not milk. The coffee adds flavor and color. Stir immediately. You want a paste that’s thick but not dry. If it’s too runny, the foam won’t hold. If it’s too dry, add a drop more coffee. The texture should resemble wet sand.</p><h3>3. Stir with Purpose</h3><p>This is where most people give up too early. Stir the paste vigorously in one direction. I use a spoon, but a small whisk works too. Keep going until the mixture turns from dark brown to a pale tan. That color change signals air incorporation. It usually takes 2 to 3 minutes. Your arm might ache. That’s normal. The foam should be thick enough to hold a peak when you lift the spoon. If it collapses, you under-stirred.</p><h2>Brewing the Coffee</h2><p>While you stir the espuma, brew your coffee. I use an espresso machine set to 92°C (198°F) with a 1:2 brew ratio — 18 grams of coffee yielding 36 grams of liquid in about 28 seconds. For a moka pot, use a fine grind (slightly coarser than espresso) and stop the brew as soon as it starts to gurgle to avoid bitterness. The coffee should be hot but not boiling — around 90°C (194°F) when you combine it with the espuma.</p><h2>Combining Coffee and Espuma</h2><p>Pour the freshly brewed espresso directly over the espuma. Do not stir yet. Let it sit for 10 seconds. The foam will rise to the surface. Then, give it one gentle stir to incorporate the foam slightly. Pour into demitasse cups. The foam should sit on top like a creamy hat. If it sinks, your espuma was too thin or your coffee was too cold.</p><h3>Common Mistake to Avoid</h3><p>Don’t stir the sugar into the entire batch of coffee. The whole point is to create a separate foam that floats. Stirring it all together dissolves the sugar completely and kills the texture. You’ll end up with a flat, overly sweet drink.</p><h2>Serving and Adjusting</h2><p>Cafe Cubano is best served immediately. The foam will start to settle after a minute or two. If you prefer a less sweet version, reduce the sugar to 15 grams — but the foam will be thinner. For a stronger coffee kick, use a darker roast with more body. Remember, caffeine affects everyone differently. If you’re sensitive, consider a single shot or a decaf version. Consult a qualified professional for personal health decisions.</p><h2>Takeaway</h2><p>The foolproof Cafe Cubano isn’t about fancy equipment or rare beans. It’s about respect for the foam. Stir long enough. Use the right sugar. Combine at the right temperature. Do that, and you’ll have a cup that’s sweet, bold, and beautifully layered. It’s a simple drink that rewards patience.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<h3>What is Cafe Cubano?</h3>
<p>Cafe Cubano is a sweet Cuban-style espresso made by mixing freshly brewed espresso with a whipped sugar foam called espuma. The sugar is stirred with a small amount of coffee until it becomes a creamy paste, then combined with the rest of the espresso.</p>
<h3>Can I make Cafe Cubano without an espresso machine?</h3>
<p>Yes, you can use a stovetop moka pot to brew a concentrated coffee. The key is to get a strong, dark brew similar to espresso. The espuma technique remains the same.</p>
<h3>What kind of sugar works best for the espuma?</h3>
<p>Demerara or raw cane sugar works best because of its coarse texture and slight molasses flavor. White granulated sugar can also work but may not produce as thick a foam. Avoid powdered sugar.</p>
<h3>Why does the espuma need to be stirred for so long?</h3>
<p>The vigorous stirring aerates the sugar and coffee mixture, creating a thick, creamy foam. This process takes about 2-3 minutes to achieve the right consistency. Under-stirring results in a thin foam that dissolves quickly.</p>

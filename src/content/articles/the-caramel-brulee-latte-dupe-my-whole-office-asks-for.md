<p>There's a particular caramel brulee latte that people line up for when the weather turns cold. The one with the crackling sugar crust, the buttery caramel that lingers, and a price tag that makes you wince. I've been making a version at home for years, and my coworkers started asking for it by name. The trick isn't in fancy equipment — it's in the caramelization and the ratio.</p>
<figure><img src="https://cdn.midjourney.com/d95716c7-3cde-4ea7-b198-764b16694781/0_2.png" alt="Homemade caramel syrup bubbling in a small saucepan, turning amber" /></figure>
<h2>The Secret Ratio for a Balanced Dupe</h2><p>Most copycat recipes use too much syrup, making the drink cloying. I keep the ratio at 1 tablespoon of homemade caramel syrup per 2 shots of stovetop espresso and 1 cup of whole milk. That's 15 ml of syrup to 60 ml of coffee and 240 ml of milk. This gives you a pronounced caramel flavor without masking the coffee's natural notes. The stovetop espresso is key here — its thicker body and lower acidity hold up to the sweetness better than a drip coffee would.</p><h2>Making the Caramel Syrup (No Corn Syrup Needed)</h2><p>You only need granulated sugar, water, vanilla, and a pinch of salt. The salt is non-negotiable — it cuts the sweetness and adds that burnt-butter edge the original is known for.</p><p>In a small saucepan, combine 2 tablespoons of sugar with 1 tablespoon of water. Heat over medium without stirring. Let it bubble. You'll see the color shift from clear to pale yellow to a deep amber. This takes about 3 to 4 minutes. Swirl the pan gently if the color is uneven, but don't put a spoon in it — that's how you get crystals. Once it's a rich copper color, pull it off the heat. Whisk in 1/2 teaspoon vanilla and 1/8 teaspoon fine sea salt. The mixture will hiss and steam. That's fine. Let it cool for a minute, then transfer to a small jar. This syrup keeps in the fridge for up to 5 days.</p><h3>Common Mistake to Avoid</h3><p>Don't let the caramel go past deep amber into dark brown. It will taste bitter and burnt, not caramelized. If you see wisps of smoke, you've gone too far. Start over — it's just sugar and water.</p><h2>The Sugar Rim: Torched or Broiled</h2><p>This is the part that makes people think you bought it. You need demerara or turbinado sugar — the coarse, golden kind. Dip the rim of your mug in a thin layer of honey or corn syrup, then into a shallow dish of the sugar. Use a kitchen torch to caramelize the sugar until it bubbles and turns golden. Let it cool for 10 seconds before pouring. If you don't have a torch, place the sugared rim under a broiler on high for 1 to 2 minutes. Watch it like a hawk — sugar burns fast.</p><p>Handle the torch or broiler with care. The mug rim gets hot. Let it cool enough to touch before handling.</p><h2>Assembling the Latte</h2><p>Brew your stovetop espresso. While it's brewing, warm the milk until steaming — around 150°F (65°C). Don't boil it; that changes the protein structure and makes frothing harder. Froth the milk with a whisk or handheld frother until you get a thick, velvety foam. Pour the hot espresso into the prepared mug. Add 1 tablespoon of the caramel syrup. Taste it — if you want it sweeter, add a little more, but start small. Slowly pour the frothed milk over the back of a spoon to create layers. Spoon the foam on top.</p><p>For the full brulee effect, sprinkle a pinch of demerara sugar over the foam and torch it again. You'll get a thin, crackly crust that shatters when you sip.</p><h2>Iced Version</h2><p>Cool the espresso and caramel syrup to room temperature. Fill a glass with ice, pour the cooled espresso and syrup over it, then add cold milk. Stir once. The sugar rim works on a chilled glass too, but the torched sugar will crack faster against the cold — that's part of the fun.</p><h2>The Takeaway</h2><p>A great caramel brulee dupe isn't about replicating a brand — it's about nailing the caramelization and balancing the sweetness against the coffee's body. Use the right ratio, torch the rim, and you'll have a drink that costs a fraction of the original and tastes like you spent the morning in a pastry kitchen. Your coworkers will ask. Be ready.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<h3>Can I use regular espresso instead of stovetop?</h3>
<p>Yes, any concentrated coffee works. Stovetop espresso has a thicker body and lower acidity, which pairs well with the caramel. Use 2 ounces of strong brewed coffee from a machine if that's what you have.</p>
<h3>How do I get the sugar rim to stick without a torch?</h3>
<p>Dip the rim in a thin layer of honey or corn syrup first, then into the sugar. The torch is only for the brulee effect. You can skip the torching altogether and still get a great crunch.</p>
<h3>Is this safe for kids or pregnant women?</h3>
<p>The recipe contains caffeine from the espresso. Caffeine affects people differently, and individual sensitivity varies. For children or during pregnancy, consult a qualified professional. Decaf espresso can be substituted.</p>
<h3>Why does my caramel crystallize?</h3>
<p>Crystallization happens if sugar granules splash onto the pan walls. To avoid this, don't stir once the sugar starts dissolving. If it crystals form, add a teaspoon of lemon juice or corn syrup to the initial mix to inhibit recrystallization.</p>

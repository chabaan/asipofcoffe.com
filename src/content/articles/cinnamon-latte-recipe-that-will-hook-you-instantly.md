<p>The first time I made this cinnamon latte, I was skeptical. Cinnamon in coffee can go wrong fast—too much powder turns it into a gritty mess; too little syrup and it's just sweet air. But I wanted a cinnamon flavor that felt real, not artificial. So I went back to basics: a whole cinnamon stick, hot espresso, and a 60-second steep. That's it. No syrups, no powders, no fuss. And honestly, I haven't made a cinnamon latte any other way since.</p>
<figure><img src="https://cdn.midjourney.com/26707f95-8fb2-4e34-ad9e-b691ff45e913/0_2.png" alt="Pulling espresso over a cinnamon stick in a cup" /></figure>


<h2>Why a Cinnamon Stick Beats Syrup Every Time</h2>
<p>Most cinnamon lattes rely on flavored syrups that are mostly sugar and artificial flavoring. They coat your tongue, yes, but they don't taste like cinnamon—they taste like candy. A cinnamon stick, on the other hand, releases essential oils when it hits hot espresso. Those oils are volatile and aromatic, giving you a clean, spicy note that lingers without sweetness. The key is the steep time: too short and you get nothing; too long and it turns bitter. I've found 60 seconds is the sweet spot for a balanced infusion.</p>

<h2>The Exact Ratio for a Balanced Cup</h2>
<p>I use an 18-gram dose of coffee, ground fine enough to produce 30 grams of espresso in about 28 seconds. That's a 1:2 brew ratio, which gives me a concentrated shot with enough body to stand up to the milk and cinnamon. For the milk, 200 grams of whole milk steamed to 150°F (65°C) creates a microfoam that's thick enough to hold a design but thin enough to pour smoothly. The final drink is about 230 grams total—espresso plus milk—which is a standard 8-ounce latte.</p>

<h3>Grind Size Matters</h3>
<p>If your espresso runs too fast (under 25 seconds), the shot will be sour and watery, and the cinnamon won't have enough intensity to shine. If it runs too slow (over 32 seconds), it'll taste bitter and over-extracted, masking the cinnamon entirely. Dial in your grind so the shot flows like warm honey—steady, not dripping.</p>

<h2>Steaming Milk: The Texture That Makes or Breaks It</h2>
<p>Steaming milk for a cinnamon latte is the same as for any latte, but the cinnamon adds a layer of complexity. You want microfoam that's silky, not bubbly. Start with cold milk in a cold pitcher. Submerge the steam wand tip just below the surface and open the valve fully. You'll hear a gentle hissing sound—that's the air being incorporated. Once the milk reaches 100°F (38°C), lower the pitcher so the tip is deeper, creating a vortex that mixes the foam into the milk. Stop at 150°F (65°C). The surface should look like wet paint, with no visible bubbles. A common mistake is overheating the milk, which kills the sweetness and creates a thin, watery texture.</p>

<h2>How to Assemble Without Losing the Crema</h2>
<p>After steeping the cinnamon stick in the espresso for 1 minute, remove it gently. The crema should still be intact—a thick, reddish-brown layer on top. Pour the steamed milk into the center of the cup from about 4 inches high. This breaks through the crema and mixes the espresso with the milk. As the cup fills, lower the pitcher and tilt it slightly to pour a white dot on top. The cinnamon flavor is now integrated throughout the drink, not just on the surface. Stir once if you want, but I prefer to leave it layered for the first sip—it's a nice contrast between the creamy milk and the spicy espresso.</p>

<h2>One Mistake to Avoid</h2>
<p>Don't add cinnamon to the milk before steaming. The heat breaks down the cinnamon's volatile oils, and you'll end up with a flat, cardboard-like taste. Also, don't use pre-ground cinnamon from a jar that's been sitting in your pantry for a year—it's lost its punch. A fresh cinnamon stick from a spice shop or grocery store is worth the extra dollar.</p>

<h2>Takeaway</h2>
<p>This cinnamon latte isn't about complexity. It's about letting one ingredient—the cinnamon stick—do the work. The hot-steep method extracts real flavor without added sugar or artificial anything. If you've been relying on syrups, try this once. I think you'll be hooked.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<h3>Can I use ground cinnamon instead of a stick?</h3>
<p>I don't recommend it. Ground cinnamon doesn't infuse cleanly—it sinks into the crema and creates a gritty texture. A cinnamon stick lets you extract the spicy aroma without the muddiness. If you must use ground, add it to the milk before steaming, then strain it out.</p>
<h3>What if I don't have an espresso machine?</h3>
<p>Use a moka pot with a fine grind. Brew 30 grams of coffee as you normally would, then pour it over the cinnamon stick. The pressure is lower, so steep for 2 minutes instead of 1. The result won't have crema, but the flavor will still be solid.</p>
<h3>How do I make this latte dairy-free?</h3>
<p>Oat milk works best because it steams similarly to whole milk and doesn't separate. Heat it to 140°F (60°C) to avoid curdling. Almond milk is thinner and won't give you the same velvety texture, but it's fine if you don't mind a lighter body.</p>
